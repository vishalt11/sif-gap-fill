\chapter{Introduction\label{cha:chapter1}}
Photosynthesis controls vegetation growth, terrestrial carbon uptake, and the formation of crop biomass, making its observation across large areas important for both carbon-cycle research and agricultural monitoring \cite{guan2016improving,he2020ground}. Conventional reflectance-based vegetation indices mainly describe canopy greenness and structure, whereas solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) is emitted by chlorophyll during photosynthetic energy conversion and provides a more functional indicator of current photosynthetic activity \cite{gensheimer2022convolutional,yu2019high}. Satellite SIF has consequently been used to study gross primary productivity, vegetation stress, and crop productivity, and it can detect heat- or drought-related reductions in photosynthetic activity before equally strong changes appear in greenness indices \cite{qiu2022monitoring,song2018satellite,peng2020assessing}. Current satellite observations, however, involve a spatial-sampling trade-off: OCO-2 provides comparatively small SIF footprints along narrow and widely separated tracks, while TROPOMI provides much more complete coverage at a coarser spatial resolution \cite{yu2019high,gensheimer2022convolutional}. Because SIF is weak relative to reflected sunlight, individual retrievals contain considerable noise, and the spatial or temporal averaging used to improve signal stability also removes detail from heterogeneous agricultural landscapes \cite{yu2019high}. Machine-learning methods can address part of this limitation by combining sparse or coarse SIF observations with spatially complete reflectance, vegetation-index, radiation, environmental, seasonal, and land-cover information \cite{li2019global,zhang2018global,gensheimer2022convolutional}. Products such as SIFoco2\_005 demonstrate that sparse OCO-2 measurements can be reconstructed as spatially continuous fields, while SIFnet shows that a spatial model can combine a parent SIF observation with finer auxiliary rasters to infer sub-grid variation \cite{yu2019high,gensheimer2022convolutional}. Nevertheless, even recent enhanced products commonly provide estimates at approximately 500~m or coarser resolution, which can remain too coarse to isolate individual crops in fragmented European agricultural landscapes \cite{gensheimer2022convolutional,du2025cnsif}. This thesis therefore investigates whether sparse OCO-2 SIF observations over Germany can be linked to Sentinel-2 data and ancillary biophysical, radiation, seasonal, and crop-composition variables represented on a 20~m grid. It compares tabular tree-based models, an area-to-point neural network, and spatial convolutional models, while separately examining how the spatial support and aggregation of OCO-2 supervision affect target stability and prediction accuracy. The resulting 20~m maps are treated as model-derived estimates learned from footprint- or grid-supported observations, rather than as directly observed or independently validated SIF at 20~m resolution. Finally, the enhanced maps are restricted to winter-wheat pixels to test whether crop-pure SIF provides more useful information for Bavarian winter-wheat yield prediction than raw OCO-2 footprints containing mixed crops and other land-cover types.
